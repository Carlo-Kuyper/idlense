# ID number research findings

While researching [`ID_NUMBERS.md`](ID_NUMBERS.md) (2026-11-16), we found places where public sources disagree with a module, or add facts that a module doesn't use yet.

## Applied on 2026-11-16

- **New documents:** `LU_MATRICULE_ENTITY`, `MD_IDNO` (check digit confirmed on 1,861 published IDNOs), `GG_TRN`, `SG_ASGD`, `SG_FOREIGN_COMPANY`.
- **Rule changes:**
  - AI_TIN prefix 1/2 with `extra.kind`.
  - AU_MEDICARE rejects issue number / IRN 0.
  - EC_RUC accepts type 6/9 mod-11 misses with `extra.checkDigitValid: false`; `identify()` then reports `checksumVerified: false` and ignores the number when guessing the country.
  - HN_DNI digits 5–8 become `extra.registrationYear` instead of the birth year.
  - SM accepts omitted leading zeros.
  - SC_TIN holder is `both`.
  - UA_EDRPOU boundary is ≥ 30,000,000.
  - US_ITIN groups 70–88 get `extra.expired`.
  - KZ_BIN AIFC serials get `extra.aifc`.
  - PE_RUC prefixes 16 and 17 are relabelled `legacy` and `legacy_or_foreigner`.
  - LA_TIN has a display format.
  - LR_TIN confidence is `moderate`.
- **Comments, sources and wording:** NZ_NHI date, NZ_IRD 200M limit (confirmed by IR, February 2026), SA check-digit note, ZA sources, GT/MN wording, FR/LK/LU/PA links.
- **Official samples added as tests:** AT, AZ, BE, BR_CNPJ, PE_RUC, MZ, SR, TW_ARC.

Everything else below is still an open lead, mostly because the only source is unofficial (e.g. ZM 13-digit NRC, NA e-ID, OM VAT `X`, NG Verhoeff, KG foreigner gender, BD smart NID, LV 9-prefix, CM `E` prefix, CL passport length, NO 2032 change). Confirm each one against an official source before changing a rule.

## AD–BO

### Disagreements with the implementation

- **AI_TIN** (src/countries/ai.ts): The current OECD sheet (https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Anguilla-TIN.pdf) says the 10 digits include "prefix digit and check digit": individual TINs start with `1`, business TINs with `2`. The module header says no structure or check digit is published, and the module accepts any 10 digits. Possible fix: pattern `^[12]\d{9}$` and extract the holder type. The check algorithm is still unpublished, so `checksum` stays false.
- **AE_EID** (src/countries/ae.ts): Some secondary sources say `YYYY` is not always the birth year (for some holders it may be the registration year): https://smarttrekassist.ae/emirates-id-format, https://dubaivisitsvisa.com/article/emirates-id. A developer gist reports real numbers failing Luhn (https://gist.github.com/geordee/e51d111426de675c0c0f8503c2003047). Microsoft Purview requires Luhn (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/sit-defn-uae-identity-card-number). ICP publishes neither rule. The module enforces Luhn and treats `YYYY` as the birth year, so it could reject valid cards. Consider extracting `birthYear` with a softer label, or noting the risk in docs.
- **BH_CPR** (src/countries/bh.ts): Wego's guide (https://blog.wego.com/bahrain-cpr-number/) says parents can pay 50–100 BD for a custom personal number for a newborn. Expat-forum reports (https://www.expat.com/forum/viewtopic.php?id=744897) show CPRs whose first digits do not match the birth year. The module's pattern requires `MM` 01–12 and reports `YY` as the birth year, so custom numbers may be rejected or misread. The sources are low quality; this is only a flag.
- **AU_MEDICARE** (src/countries/au.ts): Services Australia's operational guidance (https://operational.servicesaustralia.gov.au/public/Pages/your-health/011-44150000-01.html) says each person's IRN is 1–9 and that issue numbers go up to 9 and then wrap to 1. The module accepts `0` for both the issue number (digit 10) and the IRN (digit 11). Possible tightening: reject 0 in either position.
- **BO_NIT** (src/countries/bo.ts): The header says natural-person NITs issued since 2021 have 13 digits derived from the CI. Secondary sources (https://boliviaimpuestos.com/numero-de-identificacion-tributaria-nit/, https://lookuptax.com/docs/es/numero-identificacion-fiscal/guia-nit-bolivia) say a person's NIT is the CI digits plus 3 SIN-assigned digits, with length varying by CI. The 13-digit claim was not confirmed. The pattern (7–13 digits) covers both readings, so behaviour is unaffected; only the header text may be wrong.
- **BD_SMART_NID** (src/countries/bd.ts): bdnews24 (https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/election-commission-decides-on-10-digit-smart-nid-cards-for-bangladesh-nationals) reports the Election Commission's design: 9 random digits plus a final checksum, the first digit never 0, and no digit appearing 4 or more times. The module accepts any 10 digits, including a leading 0. This is a press report of a proposal, so treat it as a possible tightening, not a confirmed rule.
- **AR_CUIT** (src/countries/ar.ts): Some Argentine validator sites (https://hacecuentas.com/validar-cuit) list 23, 24 or 25 as the alternative person prefixes used when the check would be 10. The module accepts 25/26 for CUIT only, marked "unverified". This is consistent with the module's own caveat, and no official ARCA text was found either way.

### New facts that could improve modules

- **AZ** (src/countries/az.ts): The OECD sheet gives official samples usable as test fixtures: VÖEN `1525855852` (individual) and `1325678851` (legal entity); FİN `5VBK5VR`, `30LJV5Z`, `3C15N76`. It also states that the FİN substitutes for the TIN for individuals without one.
- **AT_UID** (src/countries/at.ts): The BMF construction rules (November 2020: https://bmf.gv.at/dam/jcr:d6794f8f-d321-43df-9840-1a841f9bf5dc/BMF_UID_Konstruktionsregeln_Stand_November%202020.pdf) give the official sample `U10223006` (check digit 6). The module computes it correctly, so it could be a fixture.
- **AT_STEUERNUMMER**: The OECD sheet confirms the 9-digit `99-999/9999` layout but mentions no check digit, and no BMF source for the Luhn rule was found. The Luhn rule is upstream-only, and `confidence: moderate` already reflects that.
- **BE_BTW**: The BMF construction rules confirm that the leading digit may be `0` or `1` from 1 January 2020 and give the sample `0776091951`. This matches the module.
- **AL_NIPT**: The OECD sheet explains that characters 3–4 are a district-dependent "converted month" (e.g. Tirana January = 13, another district January = 25). The module rightly doesn't validate them as a month. The OECD illustrative number `J12345678N` has day 45, which the module would reject; the number is only illustrative.
- **AM_PSN**: One legal site (https://armenian-lawyer.com/immigration/understanding-social-security-number-in-armenia-and-taxpayer-identification-numbers-what-they-are-for/) says a new population-register law repeals the Public Service Number law once it takes effect. This was not confirmed on arlis.am. If true, the PSN may later be marked deprecated or replaced.
- **AF_TAZKIRA**: No source other than the Landinfo note confirms the YYYY-MM layout. Public sources confirm only the 13-digit "personal file number". The low-to-moderate confidence is justified.
- **BN_IC**: The OECD sheet splits the 00 block into 250001–315000 and 315001–650000, and the 50 block into 650001–970000 and 970001–999999. These unions match the module's blocks, so there is no change.

## BR–DM

- **CM_NIU — possible third prefix `E`.** A tax-guide search result (lefisk.cm / taxdo family of pages) lists `P` particulier, `M` entreprise and `E` établissement as NIU prefixes. The module's pattern accepts only `M`/`P`. The same source's examples are 13 characters long, so it is unreliable; worth a check before widening the pattern. https://lefisk.cm/blog/numero-identifiant-fiscal-cameroun-niu-format-obtention-verification, https://taxdo.com/resources/global-tax-id-validation-guide/cameroon.md
- **CY_VAT — check-letter algorithm is not issuer-published.** The Cyprus OECD TIN sheet (28 April 2023) says only that TFA-issued TINs from 60000000 carry "a Latin character generated from an algorithm for the check character". The module documents the mod-26 rule with confidence `high`, but that rule comes from VIES/VAT validators. It still validates the example. Consider noting that the rule is unconfirmed for TFA-issued (60xxxxxx) TINs. https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Cyprus-TIN.pdf
- **CY_VAT — first digit for individuals.** The EU TIN country sheet (via search) says individuals' TICs always start with 0, 6 or 9. This agrees with the module's choice not to infer holder type from the first digit (0/9 are also used by others, and 6 now goes to everyone), so no change is needed. It is a fact worth adding to the header.
- **CN_FPRID — NIA confirmation.** The NIA adaptation guide confirms the leading `9` as a foreigner marker (citizens start 1–8), 17 digits + MOD 11-2 check, and that nationality and application-place codes are encoded. It doesn't give field widths in the snippet. The module's layout is consistent; https://www.nia.gov.cn/20231013/2.pdf could be added as a source.
- **CW_CRIB — "on request" wording.** The module header says the number is allocated "on request". The Curaçao OECD sheet says the TIN is generated by the automated system after the taxpayer's data is registered, and the taxpayer can see it only on request on tax documents. This is a minor wording difference; format (9 digits) agrees. https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Curacao-TIN.pdf
- **CL_PASAPORTE — length.** The 2024 e-passport technical specification published in the Diario Oficial gives the passport number as 9 alphanumeric characters. The module accepts 8–9, which may be right for older 2013-series passports. https://www.diariooficial.interior.gob.cl/publicaciones/2024/12/07/44018/01/2580494.pdf
- **CL_PASAPORTE — date of change.** Wikipedia says the passport number equalled the RUN until September 2013; nationid's docs say August 2013. The module says only "since 2013". No change needed.
- **BR_CNH — new source.** CONTRAN Resolution 886/2021 states that the Registro Nacional is 9 characters + 2 check digits ("dígitos verificadores de segurança"), but it does not give weights. It could be added as a source supporting the shape. https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=425117
- **BR_CNPJ — official worked example.** Receita's alphanumeric example `12ABC34501DE` gives check digits `35` (computed and confirmed: first DV sum 459 → 3, second sum 424 → 5). It is a good test fixture.
- **CA_BN — possible Luhn on the root.** Third-party validators apply Luhn to the 9-digit BN root. No CRA publication was found, so the module's shape-only check is defensible. Noted as a lead only.
- **BY_UNP — possible mod-11 check digit.** Third-party validators compute a mod-11 check for the UNP (search result on a Debian-packaged validator). No Ministry of Taxes source was found, so the module stays shape-only. Noted as a lead only.
- **BT_CID — conflicting reading of digit 1.** A tax-guide FAQ (taxdo.com) says the first digit is the dzongkhag and the next two the gewog, contradicting the two blogs the module follows. An IHSN survey codebook numbers dzongkhags differently (Bumthang 11, Chhukha 12). The module already has confidence `low` and extracts the codes raw; no change is needed, but the conflict is now noted in the doc's Caveats. https://taxdo.com/resources/faq/post/verify-bhutan-tpn-ramis-drc.md

## DO–HN

- **EC_RUC (ec.ts):** LookupTax quotes SRI's e-invoicing pages as saying RUCs of private companies, public bodies and foreigners without cédula "no tienen una validación con un algoritmo específico". The module rejects type-6 and type-9 RUCs that fail mod 11 as `invalid_checksum`, so some genuine RUCs may be wrongly rejected. Consider treating the mod-11 result for types 6/9 as advisory, or flagging it in `extra`. Source: https://lookuptax.com/docs/tax-identification-number/ecuador-tax-id-guide (citing sri.gob.ec/facturacion-electronica).
- **HN_DNI (hn.ts):** Sayari says digits 5–8 are the year the proof of birth was filed with the RNP, which can be one year later than the birth year. Digits 1–4 are the department and municipality of birth, and digits 9–13 are the volume and folio of the birth-register entry. The module returns digits 5–8 as `birthYear` and its header calls the department "of registration". Consider a field name like `registrationYear`, or noting the ±1 year in docs. Source: https://learn.sayari.com/interpreting-honduran-national-id-numbers/
- **GT_DPI (gt.ts):** Sayari says the last four digits are the department and municipality of *birth* for nationals, and of DPI registration only for naturalised citizens and permanent residents. The module header says "department of registration". This is wording only, with no logic impact. Source: https://learn.sayari.com/interpreting-guatemalan-national-id-numbers/
- **GE_TIN (ge.ts):** The OECD sheet describes the 9-digit code as "seven digits … and the codes of the taxpayer's legal forms". It does not say which position holds the legal-form code, and 7 digits plus a 1-digit code is only 8, so one digit is unexplained. The module assumes the first digit is the legal-form code and calls the rest "a seven-digit serial". Source: https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/georgia-tin.pdf
- **New fact, FO (fo.ts):** The Faroese OECD sheet also defines the TIN for legal persons as the TAKS "V number", "a consecutive number" with no published format. It is not covered. Source: https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Faroe-Islands-TIN.pdf
- **New fact, GG (gg.ts):** Guernsey's OECD sheet gives entity TIN formats that are not covered:
  - Guernsey company registration number: 1–6 digits, sequential.
  - Tax reference for non-Guernsey companies: digit + letter + 6 digits, usually with a letter suffix, e.g. `1C 123456 / O`.
  - Partnership TRN: digit + 2 letters + 6 digits + optional suffix, e.g. `1PP 123456 / F`.
  - Foundations: 1–3 digits.
  - Charities: `CH` + 1–3 digits.
  - NPOs: `NP` + 1–3 digits.
  - Trusts: report `NOTIN`.

  It also confirms the Jersey SSN shape `JY 123456 A`, which is relevant to JE_SSN. Source: https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Guernsey-TIN.pdf
- **GB_UTR (gb.ts), source disagreement, not a module bug:** An AccountingWeb forum formula, MOD(11 − MOD(sum,11),10), maps remainders 0 and 1 to 1 and 0. The module maps them to 2 and 1. A Sage worked example (`3282497350`, sum 272, remainder 8 → 3) agrees with the module but doesn't test remainders 0 or 1. HMRC publishes no specification. Sources: https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/check-digit-for-utr, https://help.sbc.sage.com/en-gb/control-centre-dt/D/CCentreUnique_Taxpayer_Reference.html
- **FR_NIR (fr.ts), confirmation:** service-public.gouv.fr confirms 91–94 for Algeria, 95 for Morocco and 96 for Tunisia for births before independence, which matches the module. The module's source URL `service-public.fr/...F33078` now redirects with a 301 to `service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33078`, so consider updating the link.

## HR–KW

- **KG_PIN, first digit 3/4 (gender for foreigners).** A web-search snippet attributed to kaktus.media (https://kaktus.media/doc/532371_kabmin_vnes_izmeneniia_v_poriadok_prisvoeniia_pin.html) says that under Resolution No. 207 of 17.04.2025 the first digit gives sex for foreigners and stateless persons too: 3 = female, 4 = male. The module reports 3/4 as `foreigner` with no gender. The article body could not be fetched, and lookuptax says foreigners get 4 only from 1 July 2025. Not confirmed; check the official resolution before changing anything.
- **IS_KENNITALA, new fact.** skra.is (https://www.skra.is/folk/eg-i-thjodskra/um-kennitolur/) says digits 7–8 are assigned sequentially starting at 20. The module accepts any RR. Not proposed as a hard rule (system kennitölur and old numbers may differ), but `serial < 20` could be a soft signal. The same page also says that when the legacy check comes out at 10, the sequence number was increased instead, which matches the module.
- **IS_KENNITALA_ENTITY, unconfirmed by the registry.** The skra.is kennitala page does not mention the day + 40 rule for legal entities. It rests on Wikipedia and practice. The module is consistent with those, but its sources don't include an official one.
- **HT_NINU, legal basis.** The module header cites a decree of 11 March 2020. A Le Nouvelliste report (https://lenouvelliste.com/en/article/262533/reforme-de-letat-civil-loni-mise-sur-le-ninu, via search summary) refers to a June 2020 decree published in Le Moniteur, which reportedly dropped the CINU/NINU acronyms. Not verified; worth checking the decree date. New facts: iciHaiti confirms the NINU is 10 digits and has replaced the NIF for passports (https://www.icihaiti.com/en/news-46606-icihaiti-flash-passport-haiti-switches-from-nif-to-ninu.html), and the ONI said it would assign a NINU automatically at birth from Q1 2026.
- **IE_PPSN, second letter B.** The module accepts A, B and H as 2013-style second letters. The sources reviewed (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Public_Service_Number) mention A (individuals) and H (non-individuals). Wikipedia also says any letter other than W may appear from 2013. B is not documented in what I read. Not a confirmed error; the module's source for B should be noted.
- **IN_PAN, status codes J and G.** Official Income Tax Department pages found in search confirm P, C, H, F, A, T, B and L. J (artificial juridical person) and G (government) were not confirmed on an official page in this pass. They are widely cited, so this is likely fine.
- **IM_TRN.** The OECD sheet text matches the module exactly, including the inconsistency it documents (text says companies use X; examples show C333333-33 for a company and X555555-55 for a trust). The sheet also says an IoM VAT number starting `100-xxxx-xxx` is not accepted as a CRS TIN. This is a new fact the module could mention; no change is needed.
- **JO_NN.** Vendor guides make conflicting claims: one says foreigners' 10-digit Personal Number starts with 9, another says the first digits encode gender (1/2). The module's example `9861012345` starts with 9, which under one of these claims would read as a foreigner's number. None of this is official; no change proposed beyond possibly choosing a neutral example.
- **JP_CORPORATE_NUMBER.** The NTA ordinance PDF in the module's sources is a scanned DocuWorks file with no extractable text, so the formula could not be re-read from it. The formula reproduces the NTA's own number (7000012050002), so it is consistent.
- **KW_CIVIL_ID.** The only source for the weights is django-localflavor; nothing official from PACI was found. The module matches localflavor.

No outright contradictions were found for HR, HU, ID, IL, IQ, IR, IT, JE, JM, KE, KH or KR. The 2020 random-digit change for KR is confirmed by 2020 Korean press (e.g. https://www.fnnews.com/news/202006291754239525). The NPWP rules match PMK 112/2022 coverage: a 0 prefix for non-individuals, mandatory from 1 January 2024.

## KZ–MN

- **LK (`LK_NIC`) — dead source link.** https://drp.gov.lk/Templates/Artical%20-%20English%20new%20number.html returns HTTP 404 (checked 2026-11-16). Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_identity_card_(Sri_Lanka)) still confirms the structure, the +500 for women, V/X and the old→new conversion (74 192 275 7 V → 1974 192 0275 7). It does not give the check-digit algorithm.
- **LB (`LB_ID`) — length/charset ambiguity.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_identity_card calls the front-of-card number "12 alphanumeric digits", but its MRZ table treats it as 12 numeric characters. The module accepts digits only. Nothing to change unless an alphanumeric specimen turns up.
- **LU — entity identifier not modelled (new fact).** The Luxembourg OECD TIN sheet (https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/luxembourg-tin.pdf) documents the 11-digit national identifier for non-natural persons (law of 30 March 1979). Its check digit is 11 − (Σ first 10 digits × 5,4,3,2,7,6,5,4,3,2 mod 11). A remainder of 0 gives check digit 0, and a remainder of 1 is never allocated. This could become an `LU_ENTITY`/TIN document. The same sheet also confirms the matricule's Luhn + Verhoeff rule over the first 11 digits. The module cites the old `oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/...` URL; the `content/dam/.../luxembourg-tin.pdf` URL works.
- **MD — IDNO not modelled (new fact).** The Moldova OECD sheet (https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/moldova-tin.pdf, Annex 2 of HG 272/2002) gives IDNO as 13 digits `1 XXX YYY ZZZZZ K`: 1 = legal-unit index, XXX = last 3 digits of the assignment year, YYY = registering office, ZZZZZ = order number, K = check digit (algorithm not given). It is the TIN for legal entities. The IDNP check digit is still described only as a "special algorithm", which matches the module.
- **KZ (`KZ_BIN`) — AIFC serial range (new fact).** The Kazakhstan OECD sheet says AIFC bodies and participants get BIN serials 90001–99999, assigned by AIFC registration authorities. The module doesn't mention or decode this; an `extra.aifc` flag would be possible. The sheet does not say a second remainder of 10 means "never issued"; ru.wikipedia does ("такой ИИН не используется"), which matches the module.
- **LV (`LV_VAT`) — unverified check on SRS-issued entity numbers.** The Latvia OECD sheet (https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/latvia-tin.pdf) says the State Revenue Service issues entity TINs of the form `9xxxxxxxxxx`, while the Register of Enterprises issues `4…`/`5…`. The module applies the weighted sum mod 11 = 3 rule to every number with first digit 4–9. No source confirms that rule for 9-prefixed SRS numbers, so they might be wrongly rejected.
- **LR (`LR_TIN`) — primary source available.** The LRA's own page (https://revenue.lra.gov.lr/?p=9216) states "It is a 9-digit number beginning with 5". The module cites only lookuptax.com and calls the claim relayed by a single secondary source. Adding the LRA page and perhaps raising confidence to `moderate` is justified.
- **LA (`LA_TIN`) — display format (minor).** Decree 354/PM art. 4 prints the TIN as `aaaaaaaab-9cc` (hyphen before the VAT digit). The module has no `format` function; adding one would match the official rendering. The structure itself matches the module.
- **MN (`MN_RD`) — letter meaning (minor).** https://www.tac.mn/article/33466 says the letters encode the aimag/district and sum/khoroo of permanent residence, and that Ч or У appear for people born in Ulaanbaatar. It doesn't settle whether they reflect residence or birthplace. The module says "of registration"; wording only.
- **MM (`MM_NRC`)** — the python-myanmar docs still restrict the state code to 1–13, while other sources allow 1–14. The module already documents this and accepts 14. No change needed.

## MO–PK

- **NZ_NHI (nz.ts header):** says the new `LLLNNLX` format applies "from July 2022". Te Whatu Ora now says the new format went live on 1 July 2026 and is only issued once the old series is exhausted (https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/for-health-providers/claims-provider-payments-and-entitlements/nhi-number-format-changes, https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/corporate-information/news-and-updates/health-new-zealand-te-whatu-ora-announces-new-national-health-index-nhi-format-effective-1-july-2026). This is a comment fix only; the logic is unaffected. HISO 10046 was also revised in 2024 and November 2025, so the module could cite the latest version.
- **NZ_IRD:** the module accepts up to 200,000,000, citing the 2026 IR payroll spec. Oracle's NZ tax docs (https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/financials/24c/faitx/new-zealand.html) and python-stdnum use 150,000,000. These are probably different spec versions rather than an error, but the bound should be confirmed against the current IR spec.
- **NO_FNR / NO_DNR:** Skatteetaten has announced new fødselsnummer and D-nummer series from 2032. The individnummer will no longer indicate the century, and the validation page mentions a "new control calculation" (https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/deling/folkeregisteret/pid/validering/, which links to source code on GitHub). The module's century table and the double mod 11 check will need revisiting before 2032. Worth reading the linked page "Om innføringen av nye fødselsnummer og d-nummer fra 2032" now.
- **NA_ID:** Namibia's new e-ID (Civil Registration and Identification Act) reportedly introduces a unique number "no longer linked to a person's date of birth" (https://itweb.africa/article/namibia-expedites-electronic-ids-issuance/j5alr7QApo87pYQk, https://www.parliament.na/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Speech_by_Hon_Kashikola_on_the_Civil_Registration_and_Identitifcation.pdf). If those numbers are also 11 digits, the module's YYMMDD date check would reject them. The module does not mention this.
- **OM_VAT:** lookuptax's Oman checker says some valid VATINs end in `X` (https://lookuptax.com/validate/oman/vat). The module's pattern `^OM\d{10}$` would reject them. This is unconfirmed by the Tax Authority.
- **NG_NIN:** Trinsic's NIN lookup docs claim the last digit is a Verhoeff checksum (https://docs.trinsic.id/docs/nigeria-nin-lookup-3). No NIMC source confirms it. If it can be confirmed against real NINs, it would add a checksum. Until then, the module's shape-only check is right.
- **PE_RUC:** Oracle's Peru docs say prefix 16 "used to be a valid number, but no longer used" and 17 covers RUCs from 1993–2000 or foreigners (https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/financials/24a/faitx/peru.html). The module labels 16/17 as "non_domiciled". The label may be inaccurate, though the logic still accepts both. Oracle's example `20503644968` (valid) / `20503644961` (invalid) is a usable test fixture.
- **NL_BTW:** business.gov.nl says the two digits after `B` in a sole trader's btw-id "are random" (https://business.gov.nl/regulations/using-checking-vat-numbers/). That doesn't contradict a mod-97 check over the whole string, but no Belastingdienst page documents the mod-97 rule. The module's `moderate` confidence fits that.
- **PK_CNIC:** Wikipedia and press reports describe the 13th digit as a "check digit" that also encodes sex (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNIC_(Pakistan)). No algorithm is published. The module treats the digit as sex only, which is consistent but worth noting.
- **PA_CEDULA source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamanian_identity_card returned HTTP 404 during this pass, so the module's source link may be dead.
- **MZ_NUIT:** python-stdnum's documented example `400339910` passes the module's algorithm (sum 209, mod 11 = 0). It could serve as an extra fixture. No discrepancy.

## PL–SV

- **SC_TIN holder (sc.ts):** the module sets `holder: "entity"` and cites only lookuptax. The OECD Seychelles TIN sheet (updated 30 May 2023), https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Seychelles-TIN.pdf, says any "person" liable for revenue must apply for a TIN under s.35 of the Revenue Administration Act 2009, and "person" includes an individual, partnership, entity, trust or government body. Only employees go without a TIN; individuals in business get one. `holder: "both"` may fit better. The sheet also confirms the 9 digits and the checksum (unpublished), so it is a stronger source to add.
- **SM_SSI / SM_COE length (sm.ts):** the OECD San Marino TIN sheet, https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/san-marino-tin.pdf, lists formats `999999999` ("more than one digit") and `SM99999`, with the comment "the first digits might be omitted if they correspond to the digit zero". python-stdnum's COE docs also say leading zeros are commonly dropped. The module requires exactly 9 digits (`/^\d{9}$/`) and exactly `SM` + 5 digits, so it rejects numbers written without leading zeros. Possible fix: accept shorter forms and left-pad them with zeros. The sheet also names the COE issuers: the Office of Industry, Handicraft and Trade, or the Labour Office.
- **SA_NIN / SA_IQAMA check digit (sa.ts):** the Saudi Central Bank rulebook, https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/entiresection/2324, says the check-digit formula is not published; entities must ask the National Information Center (Ministry of Interior) for it. The module presents Luhn as fact; only open-source validators back it. Worth a header note and this source. The same rulebook confirms 1 = citizen, 2 = foreign resident, digits 2–9 = serial, and that a resident's number equals their Iqama number.
- **PY_CI length (py.ts):** an identity-verification vendor (Didit, https://didit.me/solutions/countries/paraguay/) documents the cédula number as 6–10 digits. The module allows 6–9. This is a non-official source, so it is low weight.
- **New fact, SR_FIN (sr.ts):** an Assuria Schadeverzekering N.V. VAT certificate, https://www.assuria.sr/media/d2mpzdea/btw-certificaat-assuria-schadeverzekering-n-v.pdf, shows a 10-digit FIN, `2000002293`. This is real-world evidence for the 10-digit form the module accepts with low confidence, and a possible test fixture.
- **New fact, SO_NIN (so.ts):** stronger sources for the 11-digit length than lookuptax:
  - NIRA's ID4Africa 2025 presentation, https://www.id4africa.com/2025/files/PS1-S1-4-Somalia-FINAL.pdf.
  - The EUAA country report, which calls the number the National Identification Number (*Tirsi Aqoonsi*), issued after biometric capture under Law No. 009 of March 2023.
- **New fact, SG (sg.ts):** the OECD Singapore TIN sheet, https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/singapore-tin.pdf, adds three points:
  - IRAS-issued identifiers for those without an NRIC/FIN/UEN (ASGD `ANNNNNNNC`, ITR 10 characters) and the foreign-company form `F000NNNNNC` / `FDDDNNNNNC`. None of these are modelled.
  - NRIC numbers of those born in 1967 or earlier commonly begin with 0 or 1.
  - People who gained PR or citizenship before or after 2008 get leading digits 2/3 or 4/5.

  This is consistent with the module's decision not to extract a birth year.
- **Unconfirmed, not contradicted:**
  - SK_VAT's third-digit rule (2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9): no source found.
  - The Palestinian use of Luhn: there is no Palestinian official source. Israeli Luhn is confirmed.
  - The El Salvador DUI weights: no source found in this pass.

## SZ–ZW

- **ZM_NRC: new number format not covered.** A KYC vendor page says the Department of National Registration, Passport and Citizenship is moving to biometric cards with a new 13-digit number, while the green/pink/blue NRCs (6/2/1 format) stay in use.
